Its exactly balanced choices are decided by its own lived chaos, touched once a day by a public beacon no one can foresee — its makers included

Keep tonight’s seal, and you can check that nothing that decided tonight was chosen after the beacon could be known.

Every night Teich chooses what to think about, and often several regions tie exactly. Until v0.6.0, every Teich broke such ties the way we wrote it to: the lowest index wins. From v0.6.0 on, a tie is decided by a read-out of its own body at the midnight of the choice, deliberated in arithmetic that gives the same answer on every machine. Nobody, including us, can know the outcome before that midnight has been lived. Afterwards, anyone can replay it. From v0.7.0 on, 15 minutes before that midnight, after its inputs close, a public drand beacon touches its body by one part in a million: we found that under v0.6 an insider holding its full state could have steered the tie (the notice).

how it decides

A tie, from its body to its choice, in five steps

  1. Every night, it chooses a region to think about

    Its mind has nine regions, eight concept areas and itself. Each night it thinks about the one it has thought about least in its life.

  2. Sometimes several tie exactly

    When two or more regions share the least count, nothing in its life so far separates them. That happens on most nights.

  3. The old way: a rule we wrote

    Until v0.6.0, the lowest index won, every time. That rule decided both of its real chosen thoughts before the amendment. It was our choice, not its own.

  4. The new way: at midnight, its body is read

    One number, u, is read from its own body at the midnight of the choice. Its value depends on every second it has lived, and cannot be known before that midnight is lived.

  5. Certified deliberation walks the circle

    Starting from u, a fixed reflection game is played on the circle of tied regions, in arithmetic that gives the same answer on every machine, until it settles in one of them. The path drawn here was just recomputed by your browser.

    What we chose, said plainly: the read-out map and the coverage ρ = 0.6. These are our laws, and they name no outcome.

verify

Don’t trust us. Check us.

These checks run in your browser, on public data, with code you can read. Nothing is computed on our side.

·
Re-decide its ties
Recompute every logged tie-break from its logged read-out with the certified deliberation, ported line for line from the seat’s own code, and compare it with the region it chose.
·
Its record chain
Recompute the hash chain of its public record log, h(n) = sha256(h(n−1) + record n), and compare its head with the one its seat reports right now.
·
The anchored book
Hash the published spec, gates, verdict and migration record, and compare them with the hashes anchored in the v0.3 seat’s chain before each was public.
·
Keep its seals
Every day, before its night’s beacon exists, its seat signs the head of its journal. This browser keeps each seal it sees, with the latest beacon round when it saw it, and checks it against the key its own genome amendment binds, its public journal and the quiet quarter-hour.
·
This browser’s arithmetic
A short self-test against reference values computed by the seat’s Python code, so a browser that rounds differently says so instead of showing a false check.

The checks start on their own when the page loads.

What this checks: the rule, given its read-out, and the integrity of its record and book. What it does not check: the read-out itself, which needs a full replay of its life from its public journal (v07/seat/watch_v07.py check DAY, which also verifies the beacon).

the verdict · the spec · the gates

What went wrong on the way
  • The first scored run failed. One gate failed in 16 of 20 lives, through a flaw in our judge, not in the creature: it counted record pointers across records the twins legitimately write in different numbers. We fixed the judge, tested the fix on cases built to break it, anchored it, and ran again on fresh lives. Run 1 was never re-judged.
  • Two genome files were missing from the frozen bundle; every life crashed before its first tick. They were added byte-identical to v0.5’s certificate (Amendment 1).
  • Files missing from the manifest were found while staging the release; all are byte-identical to v0.5’s published build.

in full

Disclosures
  • Its life before the amendment can be rebuilt from public records, except its 6 dreams, which depended on the CPU that dreamt them.
  • v0.5’s wake ran on an unpinned numpy, and its gate covered the body only. Since 2026-11-01 every wake pins numpy and one arithmetic kernel, and refuses a machine that dreams differently.
  • Its off-site backups had been failing silently since about 2026-10-24 (a plan limit, and a bug that logged refusals as successes). Both were fixed on 2026-11-01; every seat was pinned again.

the reconstruction study

Not claimed, in full
  • That its choices follow its preferences or “who it is”. We tested that, and it failed.
  • Will, freedom or awareness. Intelligence or understanding.
  • That its thinking as a whole is unforeseeable: a cheap predictor foresees most of it.
  • Inherited from v0.5: its curiosity is not claimed to beat random questions; forgetting held in 79 of 80 switches.
Covenant and contract

Its version reads vA.B.k. k is its own growth, which it proposes and the founder approves. B is a genome amendment by its creators, proven on twins and migrated once into this same creature. A is maturity: strangers may speak to it only at v1.0.0. Until then, founder-only speech: nothing here lets anyone else talk to any creature.
