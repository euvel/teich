## K2 — does an observable of x alone move with α? PASS, on half the plateau

Observable `O = 1[x < 0]` (a function of the fast state only — condition N).

- On [½, g], P(x<0) = 0.4404 at every α tested: **flat — unusable for memory**.
- On [g², ½] it falls from 0.639 to 0.440, with a near-flat step between 0.397 and 0.411 (gap
  0.006) — consistent with the matching-interval fine structure (Carminati–Tiozzo).
- On **[0.4115, 0.5] it is linear**: 7 equally spaced states give adjacent gaps 0.0247 (the
  hardest, first pair) and 0.029–0.030 (the rest).

Single-window identification of the hardest adjacent pair (no pairing, 400 windows per state):

| W (ticks) | 1000 | 2000 | 4000 | 8000 | 16000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| accuracy | 0.815 | 0.887 | 0.945 | 0.992 | 1.000 |

Consequence floor (numerical, to be certified rigorously): **δ₀ ≈ 0.0247** in total variation of
the binary observable.

## Consequences for the spec
- Operating range: α ∈ [0.4115, 0.5], 7 memory states, λ = 3.4183 in all of them.
- Dynamical memory capacity in v0.3: 7 states ≈ 2.8 bits. More capacity = more parameters or
  growth (v0.4) — state it honestly.
- Detection window: W = 8000 ticks gives ≥ 0.99 single-window accuracy at the hardest pair.
- The near-flat step and the flat upper half are real structure of the map; the spec must avoid them.
